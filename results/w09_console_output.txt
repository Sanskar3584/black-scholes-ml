TASK 3  -  WHAT THE REAL RESIDUAL IS MADE OF
==============================================================================
  The benchmark uses the PREVIOUS date's at-the-money volatility,
  so the residual contains two different things. Repricing with
  TODAY's at-the-money volatility isolates them.

    total residual        mean |eps|   37.90 index points
      level part            23.97  (63%)  stale volatility
      shape part            13.94  (37%)  the smile itself
    residual is 99% one-sided

  Median at-the-money implied volatility by date:
    2026-11-13   13.75%
    2026-11-23   15.76%
    moved +2.01 points over the 10-day gap.

  THIS IS A FINDING ABOUT THE SAMPLING CADENCE, NOT ABOUT NIFTY.
  sigma_atm_prev goes stale at exactly the rate the sample is
  thinned. With weekly files the benchmark is a week old, and the
  volatility LEVEL move over that week swamps the smile shape -
  63% against 37% here. Master plan 5.5 asks the model to
  learn 'only the systematic part of the model error'. A level
  move over a week is the UNsystematic part: it is roughly a
  random walk, and no function of [m, T, r] can predict it.

  sigma_hist does not have this problem. It is computed from the
  daily NIFTY close series, which is complete regardless of how
  often the option files are sampled. That makes the week1.py
  re-run a design requirement rather than a missing column.

CHECKPOINT
==============================================================================
  Does residual learning reduce real-market Black-Scholes error?

  ON SYNTHETIC DATA, DEFINITIVELY YES. Where the model error is
  known by construction, the best hybrid cuts RMSE from 1.4218 to
  0.0572, a factor of 24.9, and beats the best pure model (0.1635)
  with the same learners. Black-Scholes supplies the structure;
  the model supplies only what the formula misses.

  ON REAL DATA, NOT YET ANSWERABLE. 2 trading dates, of which the
  first cannot be model-ready because the benchmark needs a prior
  date. 531 usable quotes is not a dataset, and with one date
  there is no chronological split to speak of.

  What the two dates DO establish is that 63% of the real
  residual is stale-volatility level error rather than smile
  shape. That is a reason to prefer sigma_hist over
  sigma_atm_prev once the window is in, and it is an argument
  the synthetic experiment could not have produced.
