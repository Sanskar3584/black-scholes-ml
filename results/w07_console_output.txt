TASK 4  -  RESIDUALS AGAINST m AND T  (master plan 11.7)
==============================================================================
  The prediction, written down before looking. The call surface
  y = C/K is convex in m: flat as m -> -inf, rising like e^m when
  deep in the money. A least-squares line through a convex function
  OVER-predicts in the interior and UNDER-predicts at both ends -
  for y = x^2 on [-1,1] the fitted line is y = 1/3, so the residual
  1/3 - x^2 is positive in the middle and negative at the ends.
  So: mean residual POSITIVE near the money, NEGATIVE in both
  wings, and |residual| largest where Gamma is largest.

  Residual by maturity (currency units at K = 100):

CHECKPOINT QUESTION
==============================================================================
  "Is the synthetic dataset traceable, and do linear models fail
   where expected?"

  Yes to both.

  TRACEABLE. Reloading the saved CSV and recomputing every stage
  from the stored input columns reproduces the file to 7.8e-14, and
  put-call parity holds on the sampled parameters to 2.8e-14 by a
  route that does not call the pricer at all. The split is a column
  in the file, so the Week 7 models will see exactly this test set.

  THEY FAIL WHERE EXPECTED. On the test split the linear model has
  RMSE 10.4565 and a worst error of 26.2419 in currency units, against a
  benchmark whose RMSE is 1.5e-14 - machine precision, not literally
  zero, because the benchmark column is recomputed from the raw
  inputs rather than copied. R^2 is 0.8230, which looks respectable
  and is exactly why R^2 is the wrong statistic here.

  The structure is the evidence, not the magnitude. Every item
  below was predicted from convexity before the fit was run:
    - mean residual near the money   +12.8524
    - mean residual in the wings     -12.4875
      a least-squares line through a convex function sits ABOVE it
      in the interior and BELOW it at both ends, so the residual
      changes sign. Predicted; observed.
    - mean |residual|, T <= 0.08       10.0952
    - mean |residual|, T in (1.0, 1.5] 7.3490
      worst at short maturity, where the surface bends most
      sharply; monotone over the first five maturity buckets,
      with a slight uptick in the last (1.5-2y, 1185 rows).
    - corr(|residual|, K*Gamma)      +0.2348
      Gamma IS the curvature in S, and the model never saw it.

  Ridge changes nothing: alpha fell to the grid floor and the test
  RMSE matches OLS to 11 significant figures. With 14,000 training
  rows and four features there is no variance to regularise. The
  error here is bias, and a penalty on the coefficients cannot
  touch bias. That is a useful negative result to have in hand
  before Week 7 reaches for more complex models.

  THE FAILURE IS THE FUNCTIONAL FORM, NOT THE FEATURES. Expanding
  the SAME four features to degree two cuts test RMSE from 10.4565 to
  2.2614, a factor of 4.6, with no new information added. The
  information needed to price the option is already in [m, T, r,
  sigma]; a linear map simply cannot extract it. That is the
  Week 7 milestone stated as a measurement.

  This sets the floor for Stage Gate 2 on 17 Nov: a tree ensemble or
  a neural network has to beat 10.4565 test RMSE by enough to justify
  its complexity, and beating 2.2614 is the more honest bar, since
  degree-two polynomial regression is nearly free.
