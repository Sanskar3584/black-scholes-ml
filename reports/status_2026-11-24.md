## What the second file was worth

It broke the pipeline, and the way it broke is worth reporting.

23 November is the first file whose trading date is also an expiry date. **On expiry day NSE
writes the underlying's final settlement value into `SttlmPric` for every expiring
contract** - 25,349.65, the same number for calls and puts across all 158 strikes of the
expiring series. It is not an option price.

`C - P` is then identically zero across that series, so the `argmin |C - P|` that picks the
reference strike selects an arbitrary one, and the implied forward comes back as that
strike:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| implied forward, expiring series | **F = 27,900** (spot 25,350) | refused |
| parity residual, median | **5,675** index points | **5.84** |
| parity residual, max | 7,050 | 331 |

What concerns me is that nothing looked wrong. The file passes checks 1 to 5, the forward is
a plausible-looking number, and only the parity residual gives it away. Three fixes are in
`implied_forward`: refuse `T <= 0`; refuse any expiry whose settlement column is constant
across strikes, which is the signature itself; and choose the reference strike only among
pairs where both legs actually traded. The fixture test plants the exact defect so it cannot
return.

## The cleaning pipeline is built and runs end to end

`clean_options.py` implements the dataset specification: per-expiry implied forward,
maturity, liquidity, out-of-the-money selection and moneyness band in the order section 6.2
requires, parity conversion of the retained puts, and no-arbitrage screening. It reports a
filter waterfall rather than only an output row count.

On the two files: 4,408 raw rows to **1,198** clean, zero no-arbitrage failures, 522 native
calls and 676 converted puts.

**The parity conversion validates.** Where the converted-put side meets the native-call side,
the implied volatilities agree to **0.1 to 0.4 percentage points** on the liquid expiries.
That is the test I most wanted to pass and it passes.

### One finding that changes the specification

The `|ln(F/K)| <= 0.3` band alone is not sufficient. Implied volatility dispersion over the
1,198 rows, by **quoted premium**:

| premium (Rs) | n | IV median | IV IQR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 - 2 | 37 | **44.77%** | 7.36 pp |
| 2 - 5 | 138 | **28.18%** | 10.55 pp |
| 5 - 20 | 244 | 20.02% | 5.85 pp |
| 20 - 100 | 404 | 16.63% | 3.09 pp |
| > 100 | 548 | **14.43%** | **1.91 pp** |

The 1 to 5 rupee band is not a smile, it is noise. The cause is the one Week 3 identified:
a deep out-of-the-money put worth Rs 0.60 converts by parity to a deep in-the-money call
worth Rs 6,453 - exact arithmetic, and still only Rs 0.60 of information.

I have added a **minimum quoted premium of Rs 5.00**, set from the principle that one tick
should be at most 1% of the quote, not fitted to these two days. It keeps 85% of rows and
cuts the overall IV interquartile range from 5.15 to 3.61 percentage points. I would like to
confirm this with you once the full window is in, since two trading dates are not enough to
settle a threshold.
